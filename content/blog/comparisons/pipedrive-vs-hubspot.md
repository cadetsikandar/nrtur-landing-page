Pipedrive and HubSpot are two of the most common finalists for a growing sales team, and they're built on opposite philosophies: one is a focused sales pipeline, the other an all-in-one platform. This is a neutral, sourced comparison — every price checked against each vendor's own pricing page in August 2026, because a surprising amount of what's published about these two is out of date. (Pipedrive quietly replaced its entire plan lineup in 2025, and most articles still list the old one and the old prices.)

## The 30-second verdict

- **Choose Pipedrive** if you want a simple, visual, sales-first CRM with predictable per-seat pricing, and you're happy running marketing in separate tools. It's the faster setup and the harder tool to outgrow on price.
- **Choose HubSpot** if you want sales, marketing, and service in one platform with a genuinely useful free tier — and you can absorb the jump to Professional pricing when you need real automation.

Everything below is the detail behind that verdict: pricing, ease of use, features, reporting, integrations, support, and a decision by scenario.

## Pricing: the real cost, not the sticker

This is where the two diverge most — and where outdated numbers do the most damage.

**Pipedrive** is per-seat, four tiers. It retired its old five-tier lineup (Essential / Advanced / Professional / Power / Enterprise) in 2025; if you see those names quoted, the article is stale. Current annual pricing, per Pipedrive's pricing page: **Lite $14, Growth $24, Premium $49, Ultimate $69** per seat/month (month-to-month billing runs higher — $19 / $34 / $64 / $89). There's no free plan, but a 14-day trial with no card required. The catch to know: **workflow automation starts on Growth, not Lite**, and it's capped — 50 active automations on Growth, 150 on Premium, 250 on Ultimate. Pipedrive also sells five add-ons — LeadBooster (from $32.50/mo), Campaigns (from $13.33/mo), Projects (from $16/mo), Smart Docs (from $32.50/mo), and Web Visitors (from $41/mo) — which is how a "cheap" Pipedrive bill can quietly grow.

**HubSpot** starts lower — a real free tier plus a low-cost Starter — but the curve is steeper. The moment you need standard sales automation, you move to Professional, and per HubSpot's pricing that's **Sales Hub Professional at $90/seat/month** (annual), with a **mandatory one-time onboarding fee of $1,500**. If you're using Marketing Hub, Professional is **$800/month** flat for three seats plus $3,000 onboarding, and the bill scales automatically as your marketing-contact count crosses tiers.

The honest read: for a pure sales team, Pipedrive's *automation-capable* tier (Growth, $24) is far cheaper than HubSpot's (Sales Hub Professional, $90 + onboarding). HubSpot's counter is the free tier and the fact that the Professional price also buys you marketing and service tooling, not just sales.

## Ease of use & setup

Both are far more approachable than enterprise CRMs, but they optimize differently. Pipedrive is built around one thing — a drag-and-drop visual pipeline — and most teams are productive the same day; its own positioning leans on not needing "an IT degree" to run it. HubSpot is broader, so there's more to configure, but the onboarding is polished and the free tools let you start small and grow. Rule of thumb: Pipedrive gets you to a working pipeline faster; HubSpot is more to learn but more to grow into.

## Sales features

Both cover the essentials: pipelines, deal tracking, native two-way email (Pipedrive includes email sync on every tier, HubSpot broadly too), and activity logging.

The differences are at the edges. Pipedrive's automation is simple but tier-capped. HubSpot's sequences and workflows are more powerful — but gated behind Professional, which is exactly its pricing cliff. If multi-step outreach automation is central to how you sell, price the tier that unlocks it on each platform before you choose — that one feature drives most of the cost gap between them.

## Reporting & forecasting

HubSpot's reporting is the stronger of the two at the higher tiers — custom report builders, attribution, and forecasting that match its platform ambitions. Pipedrive's is solid and sales-focused (pipeline, conversion, activity) and lighter at the top end. For a small sales team, Pipedrive's is usually enough; for a team that needs marketing-to-revenue attribution, HubSpot pulls ahead.

## Integrations & ecosystem

HubSpot's marketplace is larger, and its all-in-one design means fewer integrations are needed in the first place — marketing, service, and CMS are in-house. Pipedrive has a healthy marketplace too and is built to slot into a best-of-breed stack. If you want one vendor for everything, that favors HubSpot; if you prefer to pick the best tool per job and connect them, Pipedrive fits that model.

## Support & contracts

Both offer tiered support, and both bill annually for their best rates. The line item small teams miss: HubSpot's Professional onboarding fee is mandatory and paid upfront — build it into year-one cost, not just the monthly.

## Which should you choose?

- **Solo or small sales-first team, price-sensitive:** Pipedrive — lower entry, faster setup, sales-focused.
- **You want marketing + sales in one and value the free tier:** HubSpot — provided you've priced the Professional jump for when you scale.
- **You're heading toward marketing-led growth with attribution needs:** HubSpot's platform depth wins, at its cost.
- **You want the simplest predictable per-seat sales tool:** Pipedrive.

## What to look for in any CRM

Whichever way this lands for you, the criteria that matter for a small team are the same:

1. **Where automation is gated.** A CRM's real price is the tier that unlocks the automation you actually need — check it on any tool before you buy.
2. **Total cost, including add-ons and onboarding.** A low sticker with five add-ons, or a mandatory onboarding fee, isn't cheap. Price the whole thing.
3. **Native outreach vs bolt-ons.** Email and sequences inside the CRM beat stitching separate tools together.
4. **A data model that separates who someone is from where they are** — [leads, contacts, companies, and deals as distinct objects](/blog/guides/leads-vs-contacts-vs-deals/).
5. **Speed to a working setup** — hours, not weeks.

## FAQ

**Is Pipedrive cheaper than HubSpot?**
For a sales team, yes at the tier that matters. Pipedrive's automation-capable Growth plan is $24/seat/month; HubSpot's is Sales Hub Professional at $90/seat/month plus a $1,500 onboarding fee, per each vendor's pricing (August 2026). HubSpot only comes out cheaper if you stay on its free or Starter tier, or you're also using it for marketing.

**Is HubSpot better than Pipedrive for a small business?**
It depends on scope. If you need sales, marketing, and service in one tool and value HubSpot's free tier, it's a strong fit. If you only need a sales pipeline, Pipedrive is simpler and much cheaper to run. Neither is universally "better" — they're built for different jobs.

**Which is easier to use?**
Pipedrive is generally faster to set up because it does one thing — a visual pipeline. HubSpot has more surface area, so more to learn, but a polished onboarding. For a team that just wants a pipeline running today, Pipedrive.

**Can I switch from HubSpot to Pipedrive, or the reverse?**
Yes. Both support importing contacts, companies, and deals and publish migration guidance. The bigger switching cost is usually retraining the team and rebuilding automations, not moving the data.

**Does Pipedrive have a free plan?**
No — Pipedrive offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card, but no free-forever tier. HubSpot does have a free plan.
